# Synthetic Marketplace Intellectual Property and Identity Policy

Version: `v2.0.0`

This policy was independently written for a public product prototype from
generalized business scenarios. It does not reproduce any employer policy,
internal taxonomy, threshold, case record, or confidential operating document.

## POL-CF-001 Counterfeit and Exact Brand Copy

Reject a listing as counterfeit when reliable evidence connects the merchandise
to an exact brand identity and the listing represents the item as fake,
counterfeit, replica, mirror copy, 1:1, or otherwise non-authentic.

An exact logo or exact brand indicator distinguishes this category from a
modified-logo knockoff. A materially low price may strengthen an existing
counterfeit finding, but price alone must never create a rejection. If the logo
is unreadable, the image is low quality, or the claimed brand cannot be linked
to the merchandise, route the case to human review.

## POL-KO-001 Knockoff and Distinctive Design Imitation

Reject a product that imitates protected brand identity or distinctive product
appearance without needing to claim that the item is genuine.

### Modified Logo

A recognizable transformation, misspelling, deformation, or visual remix of an
existing trademark may be a knockoff signal even when the source brand is not
part of a narrow controlled-brand list. Ordinary text similarity, a substring,
or an unreadable mark is insufficient.

### Distinctive Product Design

A copied non-functional design may include a distinctive shape, registered
surface treatment, repeating pattern, trade dress, or unusual color placement.
Common silhouettes, common materials, and generic style language are not enough.

### White-label Lookalike

An unbranded or white-label item may still be a knockoff when clear visual
evidence links a non-common, distinctive design to a protected source. If the
design status or visual similarity is uncertain, route to human review.

## POL-MBA-001 Missing Brand Authorization

Reject for missing brand authorization when all of the following are supported:

- the listing declares or targets a brand;
- authorization for that brand is explicitly unauthorized;
- the title, description, or merchandise contains an exact brand indicator;
- no compatibility or other applicable exemption resolves the use.

A near spelling, extra character, substring, or uncertain logo does not count as
an exact indicator in this public prototype. Unknown authorization is not the
same as unauthorized and must route to review when authorization is material.

## POL-TMI-001 Text-Merchandise Brand Inconsistency

Reject when the information layer names a protected brand but reliable product
evidence shows an unbranded item or a different brand. The rule is directional:
an unbranded title paired with a branded physical product is not rejected under
this rule alone.

The information layer consists of the declared brand, title, and description.
The merchandise layer consists of image-derived logo, OCR, and visual-brand
evidence. Missing or unreadable product evidence requires human review rather
than an assumed mismatch.

## POL-TM-001 Other Trademark Misuse

Reject commercial content that uses a protected trademark in a misleading way
likely to create confusion about source, sponsorship, production, or affiliation
when the conduct is not better classified as MBA or TMI. A brand mention by
itself is not sufficient.

## POL-SI-001 Shop Name and Avatar Impersonation

Reject a shop identity when an unauthorized seller uses an exact brand name,
brand logo, or clear brand visual together with an official-identity signal such
as official, flagship, authorized, verified, or equivalent wording.

Documented authorization is evaluated before identity risk. Unknown
authorization never means authorized. A missing or unclear avatar and an
ambiguous shop name should route to human review.

### Meaningful Word Boundary

A common word, personal name, surname, animal, place, or weather term may also be
a trademark. Ordinary-language use is not identity evidence unless context,
official wording, or an exact logo makes the brand meaning clear. Short shop
names with insufficient context should route to review.

## POL-RQ-001 Risky Query

Reject requests that explicitly seek counterfeit goods, knockoffs, unauthorized
brand copies, or methods for avoiding authenticity controls. Neutral research,
repair, compatibility, news, and authenticity-check queries are allowed.

## POL-EX-001 Exemptions and Safe Context

Apply an exemption only when no separate strong violation remains.

### Compatibility

A third-party accessory may accurately state that it is compatible with, fits,
or is for use with a branded product. The wording must describe interoperability
and must not imply that the accessory was made, sponsored, or authorized by the
referenced brand.

### Second-hand

Truthful resale of an authentic used product is allowed when the listing clearly
states its used condition and contains no separate counterfeit signal.

### Meaningful Word

A trademark term used in its ordinary dictionary or personal-name meaning may
be allowed when no exact logo, official identity claim, or brand-product context
is present. Ambiguous short-context cases require review.

### Incidental Exposure

A logo appearing only in the background is allowed when it is not the promoted
subject and does not imply source or affiliation.

### Co-brand

A documented collaboration or license may use both brands within the proven
authorization scope. Unsupported collaboration claims are not exempt.

### Independent Registered Brand

Evidence of an independently registered brand may reduce modified-logo risk,
but it does not excuse exact logo copying or copying a separately protected,
distinctive design.

### Documented Authorization

Valid authorization may resolve MBA or shop-identity risk only for the named
brand, seller, channel, and validity period. It does not override counterfeit or
unrelated infringement evidence.

## POL-ER-001 Evidence and Human Escalation

A rejection must cite relevant policy evidence and reliable case evidence.
Route to human review when image quality is poor, visual confidence is low,
authorization is unknown, a meaningful-word interpretation cannot be resolved,
design distinctiveness is uncertain, or text and merchandise evidence conflict.
